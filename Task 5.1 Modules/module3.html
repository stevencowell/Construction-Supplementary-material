Observation Checklist Part D — Production Requirements

Part D covers the live bricklaying project. Before the assessor signs off, you must show that you have reviewed every planning document and are applying them while you work.

  • Access the project plans supplied by your teacher and confirm wall dimensions, openings, lintels, and control joints.
  • Open Section 2 Part A — Work Plan (bricklaying project) so you can explain the sequence, inspection hold points, and coverage calculations.
  • Re-read the Employee Handbook extracts: Preparing to Lay Bricks and Blocks, Mixing Mortar, Undertaking Bricklaying, and Mortar Joint Finishes. Quote the relevant steps as you complete them.
  • Keep the cement mixer SOP/operator manual with your SWMS. Reference the checklist before every batch, especially guarding, isolation, and cleaning steps.

Teacher instruction prompts (Part D)

Capture the guidance your teacher provides on:

  • Mixing mortar to specification.
  • Loading mortar boards and staging materials at height.
  • Blending and laying bricks/blocks to the nominated bond.
  • Jointing brick joints and achieving the required finish.

Document these notes in your site diary or work plan; the assessor may ask how you applied the advice mid-task.

Setting Out & Control

Observation Part D starts before the first brick is laid. You must prove that your wall position, levels, and gauges agree with the plan and the Section 2 Part A work instructions.

  • Establish profiles at corners, plumb them, and confirm the string lines clear the wall face by 3-5 mm.
  • Mark course heights on gauge rods for both bricks and blocks, allowing for damp-proof course, lintel seatings, and joint thickness.
  • Check diagonals for squareness and confirm cavity widths or wall thicknesses match AS 3700 requirements.
  • Record tolerances in your notebook; assessors often ask for the acceptable deviation (for example, +/- 4 mm in 3 m for plumb).

Brick Terminology Visual Check

Assessors often point to a labelled brick and ask you to identify the face or edge before you can move on to cutting tasks. Use the diagram below as your revision aid.

Definitions adapted from the Study Made Easy brick masonry guide (2025). Image sourced from the provided Task 5.1 photo pack.

Bed / Face

The top or bottom surface of the brick that receives the mortar bed. Keep it clean and dampen lightly before laying.

Stretcher

The long face of the brick. Visible when bricks are laid lengthwise; must align with the string line.

Header

The short face at the end of the brick. Used to tie wythes together and when forming returns.

Arris

The sharp meeting edge of two faces. Protect arrises from chips during handling and tooling.

Frog

The recess on the bed that reduces weight and improves mortar bond. Keep frogs up unless plans specify otherwise.

  1. Close the labels on the diagram and call out the bed, header, stretcher, arris and frog for a peer to confirm.
  2. Explain how damaging each feature (for example chipping an arris) affects Observation Part D quality checks.
  3. Match each feature to the related handbook instruction so you can quote the page when asked.

Mortar Mixing Excellence

  1. Complete the cement mixer SOP checklist: isolation test, guard inspection, RCD check, drum cleanliness, and emergency stop test.
  2. Measure materials by bucket (cement) and shovel (sand) ratio - commonly 1:4 with plasticiser or lime as specified.
  3. Add half the water first, then cement, then sand. Allow the drum to mix for at least three minutes.
  4. Adjust water gradually until the mix can be picked up on a trowel without slumping but still spreads easily.
  5. Load mortar boards evenly and scrape down the drum before cleaning to avoid buildup.
  6. Discard mortar after 60 minutes or once it has been retempered.
Quality check: A firm mortar bed should support a brick without sinking more than 3 mm. Remove rejected mixes from the work zone immediately.

Reference the Mixing Mortar and Undertaking Bricklaying handbook pages as you demonstrate each step to the assessor.

Laying Sequence

Follow a predictable workflow so the assessor can observe every element.

  • Lay leads (corners) first to full height or at least five courses so you can run the string line.
  • Butter vertical joints with the trowel tip, press the brick into the mortar bed, and tap down to line with the string.
  • Maintain consistent perp joint width (usually 10 mm). Use your gauge rod often, not just the string line.
  • For blockwork, fill cores according to the plan (for example every second core with grout or reinforcement).
  • Check alignment front and back - walls must be straight on both faces.

Joint Finishing & Tolerances

Joint finishing demonstrates workmanship and weatherproofing. Time it correctly:

  • Strike or iron joints when mortar is thumbprint hard (surface sheen gone but still workable).
  • Clean excess from arrises using a soft brush and avoid smearing mortar down the wall.
  • Finish perp joints first, then bed joints, to avoid tearing the mortar.

Cross-reference the Mortar Joint Finishes pages in the Employee Handbook to ensure your technique matches the required profile (e.g., ironed, ruled, raked).

Level of bed joints +/- 3 mm in 2 m Use a level each course and make corrections immediately.
