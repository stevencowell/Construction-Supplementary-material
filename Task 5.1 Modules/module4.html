Observation Checklist Part E — Documentation First

Part E occurs after the bricklaying project is complete. Before you touch any equipment, demonstrate that you have reviewed the required references:

  • Open the Brick and Block Laying — Brick and Block Clean Up section of the New Place Construction Employee Handbook and highlight the PPE, disposal, and storage instructions.
  • Access the operator manuals/SOPs for all plant, tools, and equipment you used (cement mixer, wet saw, grinders). Confirm the shutdown, cleaning, and storage steps that apply.
  • Note any maintenance tasks required (blade changes, lubrication, cord replacements) and prepare the Asset Maintenance Register for completion.
Teacher instruction prompts (Part E):
  • Cleaning the work area and disposing of materials without harming the environment.
  • Disconnecting, cleaning, and storing electrical equipment safely.
  • Cleaning, checking, maintaining, and storing hand tools and plant ready for the next user.

Listen carefully and record each instruction in your site diary. The assessor may ask how you followed the teacher’s directions during cleanup.

Wet Cut Brick Saw Procedure

  1. Complete a pre-start: inspect blade condition, guard alignment, water feed, and RCD before energising.
  2. Position the saw on stable ground with drainage or containment to capture slurry.
  3. Wear the required PPE - eye protection, hearing protection, gloves, and waterproof apron/boots.
  4. Adjust the fence or guide to hold the brick or block firmly. Use two hands and keep fingers clear of the blade path.
  5. Allow the blade to reach full speed before feeding the material slowly. Do not force the cut.
  6. After use, isolate at the switch and unplug, then wash down the bed and remove sludge to the designated waste tub.
  7. Sign off the maintenance register if you replaced a blade, repaired a guard, or noted faults needing follow-up.
Remember: Silica dust is a respiratory hazard. Ensure the water feed is continuous and never dry-cut masonry on this task without written approval. Log any dust-control issues in the site diary and report them to the assessor immediately.

Brick Saw Cut Types You Must Demonstrate

Observation Parts C and E require you to produce common bats and closers on the wet saw and explain their purpose. Practise each cut until you can name it from a labelled image and describe how it keeps the bond running correctly.

Cut / Closer Description & Use Saw Setup Checklist
Half bat Brick cut across its width so the length is half. Used to break bond at returns and to start alternate courses. Mark 110 mm from the header, square up with a bolster, then use the saw fence to hold the stretcher face flat.
Three-quarter bat Length equals three quarters of a full brick. Maintains lap where openings interrupt the pattern. Measure from the header end, lock the mitre gauge at 90°, and feed slowly to avoid over-cutting the arris.
Queen closer Brick cut longitudinally into two equal widths. Placed next to quoin headers to stop vertical joints lining up. Lay the brick on its bed, clamp against a guide block, and run the saw parallel to the stretcher face for the full length.
King closer Width is halved at one end but full at the other. Provides a half-header/half-stretcher face at corners. Mark a diagonal from the centre of one end to the adjacent side, tilt the fence accordingly, and keep hands behind the cut.
Bevelled closer A king closer with the entire stretcher face splayed. Used where the bond needs a gradual change in width. Set the saw table to the required bevel (45–60°), confirm the guard still covers the blade, and make two light passes.
Mitred closer End is mitred across the full width for angled junctions. Common for sill returns and reveals. Lock the mitre gauge to the requested angle, brace the brick with both hands, and let the blade reach full speed before feeding.
Dogleg / angle brick Factory or site-cut brick for non-90° quoins. Prevents gaps in the bond when walls change direction. Template the required leg lengths, mark both faces, complete the first rip, rotate, then finish the return cut while supporting the waste.

Cut definitions adapted from the Study Made Easy brick masonry guide (2025). Bring the page reference to the observation so you can cite it when questioned.

  • Label an offcut tray with each closer/bat name and place your samples there for the assessor.
  • Call out the measurement and intended location (e.g. "queen closer at the quoin") before you cut.
  • Check that every sample meets tolerances with a tape or combination square, then record it in your site diary.

Manual Cutting & Shaping

Where bolsters or grinders are authorised:

  • Score layout lines on both faces before striking to reduce shattering.
  • Use clamps or a vice to secure small units.
  • Keep spare blades/discs stored dry and inspect for cracks before fitting.
  • Collect offcuts for either reuse (half bricks) or safe disposal - no debris left on scaffolds or access paths.

Cleanup & Waste Segregation

Observation Part E assesses how well you return the work area and equipment to service.

  • Follow the Brick and Block Clean Up handbook section step-by-step: don the prescribed PPE, isolate equipment, and lay out waste tubs before you start cleaning.
  • Scrape leftover mortar into tubs; never hose it into drains. Hardened waste can be broken down for recycling.
  • Wash mixers, trowels, and boards with minimal water, then dry, oil lightly, and store in the allocated racks.
  • Separate waste streams: bricks/blocks, metals (ties), timber pallets, plastic strapping, and slurry.
  • Complete the operator-manual shutdown steps for each tool (wet saw, grinder, mixer) including cord checks, blade inspections, and guards.
  • Document maintenance performed (blade change, mixer lubrication) in the Asset Maintenance Register.

Final Quality Inspection

Complete a self-check before inviting the assessor to sign off:

  • Measure wall plumb/level against tolerances. Record readings for your portfolio.
  • Confirm control joints, weep holes, flashings, and damp-proof courses are clean and unobstructed.
  • Brush down the wall and repair any chips or smears. Replace damaged units immediately.
  • Photograph the completed work and upload to your digital log with notes on mortar class, weather, and curing instructions.
  • Close out paperwork: update the maintenance register, file SOP checklists, and note the cleanup completion in your site diary for the assessor.
