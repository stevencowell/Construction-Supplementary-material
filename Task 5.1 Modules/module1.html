Observation Checklist Part A — Simulation Focus

Part A is delivered through targeted simulations so the assessor can watch you manage uncommon or high-risk situations.

  • Critical events (e.g., fire evacuations) rarely happen on cue, so simulations prove you can respond even when the workplace is quiet.
  • Some scenarios require resources your workplace may not own, such as barricading equipment or spare plant for fault finding.
  • Other hazards (like asbestos identification) cannot be safely recreated in a live setting, so the simulation is the only safe space to practise.

Treat the simulation like a real shift: speak to the assessor as if they are your supervisor, complete paperwork authentically, and reference the same policies you would use on site.

Student Procedure — Part A

  • Access the Employee Expectations section of the New Place Construction Employee Handbook before the observation begins.
  • Re-read the pages titled Equipment Maintenance and Breakdowns so you know how to isolate plant, log maintenance requests, and escalate unresolved faults.
  • Revise the Material Deliveries pages, highlighting delivery booking steps, exclusion zone requirements, and documentation you must sign off during the simulation.

Legislation & Australian Standards

Brick and blocklaying work is controlled by several overlapping frameworks. You must understand how each one shapes planning, supervision, and documentation.

  • AS 3700 Masonry Structures: Governs structural design, tolerances, control joint placement, reinforcement, and mortar classes.
  • AS 4773 (Masonry - Small Buildings) clarifies workmanship tolerances for low-rise projects, including alignment, plumb, and blockwork dimensions.
  • Work Health and Safety Act & Regulation 2011 (NSW): Sets the legal requirement to identify hazards, consult workers, and keep Safe Work Method Statements (SWMS) for high-risk construction work.
  • National Construction Code (NCC) Volume 2: Links building classifications to masonry performance requirements such as fire resistance levels, waterproofing, and articulation joints.

Key Site Documents

Observation Part A focuses on how you prepare paperwork before work starts. Know the role of each document and where to find the information you need.

Document quick guide

  • Teacher-supplied project plans: Provide wall dimensions, gauges, and hold points you will be assessed against.
  • Section 2 Part A — Work Plan (bricklaying): Lists the sequence, mortar class, and inspection requirements for the brick project.
  • Section 2 Part B — Work Plan (blocklaying preparation): Breaks down staging, layout checks, and coverage requirements for blocks.
  • Brick and Block Coverage tables: Support your material calculations so you can justify delivery sizes and mortar batches.
  • Job Safety Analysis (JSA): Breaks the task into steps, lists hazards, and selects controls referencing the hierarchy.
  • SWMS: Mandatory for brick/blocklaying because it involves work at height, manual handling risks, and plant. It builds on the JSA and must be signed before work starts.
  • Safe Operating Procedures (SOPs): Provide machine-specific instructions (e.g., wet cut saw, cement mixer) that must be read and attached to the JSA bundle.
  • Employee Handbook extracts: Outline site-specific policies such as "Equipment Maintenance and Breakdowns" and "Material Deliveries" that Part A scenarios reference.

Observation Checklist Part B — Planning Requirements

Before the assessor signs you onto the preparation tasks, you must demonstrate that you have reviewed the entire planning pack and understand how to apply it.

  • Access the project plans supplied by your teacher so you can quote dimensions, bond patterns, and control joint locations.
  • Reference AS 3700 when selecting mortar classes, reinforcement, and workmanship tolerances.
  • Cross-check Section 2 Part A (bricklaying) and Section 2 Part B (blocklaying preparation) work plans to confirm sequence, task allocation, and inspection hold points.
  • Review the Preparing to Lay Bricks and Blocks and Brick and Block Coverage pages in the Employee Handbook so you can justify material quantities.
  • Gather the SOPs and operator manuals for every plant item you intend to use (cement mixer, wet saw, hoists, etc.) and attach them to your JSA/SWMS.

Teacher briefing topics to capture

  • Project possibilities and any variations you must be ready to discuss.
  • Preparing plant, tools, and equipment so they meet WHS and manufacturer requirements.
  • Setting up a cement mixer, including electrical checks and guarding.
  • Setting up a raised mortar board or hop-up safely and within reach limits.
  • Preparing the work area, including barricades, signage, and access routes.
  • Checking material quantity and quality the moment deliveries arrive.

Consultation & Reporting

Every observation requires you to demonstrate effective communication. The assessor looks for evidence that you:

  • Conduct toolbox talks that cover hazards, weather, deliveries, and neighbour impacts.
  • Escalate defects (damaged pallets, faulty mixers, missing PPE) through the maintenance register.
  • Confirm access routes and exclusion zones with supervisors before unloading bricks or blocks.
  • Record attendance and signatures on SWMS/JSA paperwork and file them in the site folder.

Site Readiness Checklist

Use this checklist before the assessor begins Observation Part B:

  • Verify services (power, water) and emergency access are available and clearly marked.
  • Measure the work area for scaffolds, hop-up brackets, and raised mortar boards; confirm load ratings.
  • Stage deliveries: count packs, inspect pallets for damage, and isolate suspect loads to prevent collapse.
  • Complete cement mixer and other plant pre-starts in line with each SOP, including electrical isolation, guard checks, and a controlled test run.
  • Set up raised mortar boards and hop-ups to the heights specified in Section 2 Part A/B so trowels, levels, and gauges are within safe reach.
  • Map prevailing weather and environmental controls - dust suppression, wash-out areas, stormwater protection.
  • Update the site diary with planned brick/block quantities, mortar class, and curing/inspection hold points.
